Small Studios, Big Flavors: The Indie Game Life

← Back to main page

02.11.2026

Not every game is made by a thousand people in a glass tower. Some are made by a handful of people, a lot of coffee and one computer that sounds like a jet engine every time the project opens.

That's indie game development. No publisher telling you what's trendy, no committee voting on the color of a button. Just a small team making the game they actually want to play, and hoping a few other people want to play it too.

It has never been a better time to try. Engines are free, digital stores are open to everyone, and a game made in a bedroom can end up on millions of screens. Some of the most loved games of the last decade started exactly like that.

It's not all sunshine, of course. Indie developers are also the artists, the testers, the marketing department and the person who fixes the website at midnight. Thousands of new games come out every year, and getting noticed is half the battle.

But the freedom is worth it. Indie games get to be weird, personal and brave. A bug that can't fly? A galaxy run from a phone? Why not.

That's the flavor we're here for. Thanks for stopping by.
